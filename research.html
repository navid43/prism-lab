Research

Light, tissue, and computation.

We work across optical modeling, measurements, and image reconstruction. Our goal is to make biomedical optical imaging more quantitative and dependable.

OUR FOCUS AREAS
01 / DIFFUSE OPTICAL IMAGING

Seeing through scattering tissue.

Diffuse optical tomography (DOT) measures light after it has traveled through tissue. Because the photons scatter many times, recovering a reliable image requires a model of light transport and a carefully designed inverse problem.

We explore Monte Carlo simulations, anatomical information, and reconstruction algorithms to estimate properties such as absorption and scattering. Breast tissue modeling and hemoglobin-related contrast are important application areas.

02 / FLUORESCENCE IMAGING

Beyond fluorescence intensity.

Fluorescence intensity does not tell the whole story. Spectral response and fluorescence lifetime offer additional information about probes and their local environment.

Our work examines fluorescence-based optical measurements, fluorophore characterization, and the effects of spectral uncertainty. We are interested in methods that connect laboratory measurements to more reliable tissue imaging.

Fluorescence lifetimeSpectroscopyFluorophoresMeasurement uncertainty
03 / COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

Reconstruction guided by physics.

Optical inverse problems can be ill-posed: different tissue-property distributions may explain similar measurements. We study ways to combine physical models, numerical optimization, and machine learning.

Our interests include simulation-based training data, uncertainty in recovered parameters, and learning methods that account for tissue heterogeneity and model limitations.
